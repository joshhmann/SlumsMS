# 1.5.0 deploy notes: bot drama (branch `bots-drama-150`)

Romance between players and bots (opt-in), spicy rivals, guild wars. Owner's call 2026-10-22 ("spicy"). For the rc4 test
install first, then live with the owner's go. Nothing here has been run on a server yet.

Code: `src/main/java/soloMapling/ArtificialPlayer/BotDramaSystem/`, lines in `BotDialoguePack/DramaDialogue.yaml` and
the `Drama*` nodes of `MegaphoneDialogue.yaml`. Ticket: [58](../tickets/58-bot-affinity.md) (bot drama section).

## Guardrails (owner decisions, kept exactly)

- `@botdrama off` opts a player out of all rival, KS, callout and guild-war drama aimed at them (and of the loud
  romance drama: their breakups are a quiet whisper, no megaphone, the ex never turns rival, no wedding objection).
- Romance needs its own opt-in, `@romance on` (off by default, stored per character). Without it no bot flirts with,
  dates or proposes to the player; the chat prompt tells the bot not to flirt and a flirty model reply falls back to a
  scripted line.
- Content: romance is flirty, cheesy, dramatic, PG-13; trash talk is about the game only. Enforced in the prompts and
  by `ContentGuard` on every model reply (`ReplyCleaner.voiceProblem`) and on every scripted drama line (a test reads
  the packs too). No emojis: the v83 client cannot draw them, so the breakup megaphone says `</3`.
- Every megaphone goes through `WorldEventMegaphones` and so `BotMegaphoneGate` (one bot megaphone per 120 s world-wide)
  and `@quiet megaphones`.
- No griefing: everything is rate limited and time limited; nothing follows a player map to map except a partner
  (romance, opt-in, at most every 15 min) and one revenge follow (rivals).

## Migrations (stop the game, back up first; idempotent, with rollbacks)

- `015-bot-romance.sql`: tables `botromance` (one row per player and bot they dated: stage, times, ring kind, the
  bot's ring id) and `botromanceopt` (who turned `@romance on`). Ledger id `015-bot-romance`. Rollback
  `015-bot-romance.rollback.sql`: drops both and also frees any player engaged or married to a bot the stock way (their
  `marriages` row and `rings` pair go, ring items become plain rings, `characters.partnerId/marriageItemId` reset), so
  an older jar can still log them in.

- `016-bot-guild-wars.sql`: table `botguildwar` (one row per war or alliance: the two guilds, whether the second is a
  player guild, state, times, turf scores). Ledger id `016-bot-guild-wars`. Rollback `016-bot-guild-wars.rollback.sql`
  drops it (guilds and members untouched).

Without 015 / 016 the server runs: romances and wars live in memory until a restart (one warning each: "is migration
015-bot-romance applied?", "is migration 016-bot-guild-wars applied?").

## Core code touched (hooks only, each behind `MapleRootSolo.runHook` or a bot check)

- `RingActionHandler`: a ring-box proposal to a bot goes to `BotRomance.proposalToBot`; a bot's own proposal answered
  in the stock window goes to `BotRomance.playerAnsweredBot`; a stock divorce / broken engagement with a bot partner
  calls `BotRomance.onStockBreak` instead of the offline `characters` update.
- `SpouseChatHandler`: a bot spouse (or fiance) answers through the chat brain; offline, the stock "Your spouse is
  currently offline".
- `Character.getNameById`: a bot id answers with the bot's name (a bot has no `characters` row; the ring and marriage
  packets would otherwise get a null name for a bot spouse and fail).
- `GeneralChatHandler`: a line that answers a drama scene ("yes" to a bot asking you out, "i do", a demanded "sorry")
  is not also routed to the bots' chat.
- `MapleMap.addPlayer`: `BotDrama.onPlayerEnteredMap` (a partner follows now and then, a rival follows once).
- `BotConversation`, `BotPlayerReaction`, `BotPopulationDirector` (step; a bot in a drama scene is never logged out),
  `BotGeneration` (a bot engaged or married to a player takes up its partner id and ring at login), `BotCouples` (a
  bot with a player never joins a bot couple; its wedding ring is not swept off).

## Romance (`@romance on`)

- Dating: a friend bot that likes the player a lot (affinity 45+) flirts; at 55+ it may ask them out ("yes"/"no" in
  map chat). A player asks a friend bot out ("wanna go out with me?"): yes at 35+. A bot dates one player at a time and
  never while in a bot couple. A partner logs in now and then when its player is online (70%, at most every 30 min) and
  comes to their channel; follows them to a new map (35%, at most every 15 min; a training partner trains there 10
  min); sits with them on a couple chair; whispers something cute (at most 4 a day per player, 40 min apart, 30 an hour
  world-wide, never while the player trades, shops, talks to an NPC, is in an event or mid-chat with a bot); gets
  jealous of the player's rivals on the map (30 min gap) and of other bots whispering them (2 h gap).
- Marriage: the stock ring-box proposal works on a bot partner (any genders; stock level 50 rule), or the partner
  proposes after 2 days of dating and the player accepts in the stock window. Engagement = the stock rows (marriages,
  tokens, partner ids). `@romance wedding` in Amoria: a staged ceremony (the stock Cathedral/Chapel instances need two
  real players) with the player's online friend bots as guests plus new faces; at "i do" it writes exactly what the
  stock vows step writes (`Ring.createRing`, the player's ring with its ring id, both marriage rings and marriage item
  ids). Divorce: drop the ring (stock) or `@romance divorce` (ring in the equip inventory).
- Offline bot spouse: spouse chat says "offline" (stock), the ring look and the character info name the bot
  (`getNameById`), the bot takes up its partner id and ring at its next login.
- Drama: neglect (4 days without standing on one map: a warning whisper, then 30 min later the breakup), the breakup
  megaphone `I'M DONE WITH YOU <name> </3`, the ex turns rival and trash-talks (game only), rebounds with another bot
  (a bot couple), comes back as an old flame after 60 days. Dating two bots: 20 min after the second starts, with both
  online, they confront it on the player's map in public chat and one (sometimes both) dumps the player. Gossip: a bot
  watching the couple writes "saw X with Y at <map>" into its memory (gossip spreads it) and tells its guild (6 h gap
  per couple). Weddings: an ex (or a bot with a crush) shouts I OBJECT (35%), the partner runs from the altar (8%: no
  marriage, still engaged), an ex or rival crashes the reception (30%). A married pair never breaks up on its own.

## Spicy rivals (bot drama on; `@botdrama off` removes all of it)

- Roasts: a rival's taunt (ticket 58's on-sight taunt, at most every 20 min per pair) is built from real facts about the
  player's game - level against the map's mob level ("lv 45 still in ant tunnel lol"), deaths today, a weapon 25+
  levels under them, KS count, job. Half the time, with the chat model live, it is one model line from those facts
  under the game-only rules (`RivalRoasts.prompt`), dropped for a scripted roast if `ContentGuard` objects.
- KS wars: a rival training bot on the player's map may call 2-4 guildmates (else its partner, else free training bots
  on the channel, never the player's friends) - nemesis 15% / rival 5% per sighting, at most once per player per day
  and 3 an hour world-wide. They come to the player's map next to them, each takes up to 5 of the player's mobs within
  its own reach for 3 minutes (ticket 58's KS spree rules), then say something smug and go back where they were.
- Callouts: a nemesis (or a rival the player KS'd 4+ times) may post "X is a KSer, dont party with them" by megaphone:
  once per player per day, 4 an hour world-wide, through the gate.
- Revenge arcs: after a taunt or KS spree the rival is armed for 10 min; when the player takes a portal it follows
  once (50%, once per pair per day, 6 an hour world-wide) to the new map and demands an apology. "sorry" (rules, else
  the Decide model's apology/mocking question) calms it (+30 affinity); mocking makes it a nemesis; 90 s of silence
  and it gives up. Then it goes back. Nothing else follows a player.

## Spicy guilds (bot guilds)

- Wars: a rival pair of bot guilds declares war (about 0.5 a day per pair, at most 2 bot wars at once, 3 days apart)
  with a megaphone and guild chat. For 7 days, every minute, each grind map where members stand is held by the side with
  more of them there (+1 point). Every ~3 h a war megaphone (score, a held map); every ~30 min a turf fight (a grinder
  goes to a map the other side holds, 20 min). At the end the winner's megaphone.
- War on a player guild: every 30 min per bot guild, a player guild whose online members (with bot drama on) are half
  or more its rivals gets a war (50%, 2 an hour world-wide, 3 days apart): a megaphone and a `[Guild War]` notice to the
  members. Turf counts the players too; the bot guild's grinders come to where its players grind and jab them (never
  one who opted out). `@guildwar` shows the score, days left and held maps; `@guildwar peace` (master or jr. master)
  ends it at once.
- Poaching: per bot guild with a rival, every 6 h at most (30%), a rank 3+ member of the rival switches to it with
  guild chat drama (and a megaphone 1 in 4). Players in player guilds who are friends of an online member get a whisper
  offer (once a week per player, 2 an hour world-wide; never `@botdrama off`); leaving their guild within 15 min brings a
  real invite.
- Alliances: every 12 h a 30% roll allies two non-rival bot guilds (at most 2 alliances): megaphone, guild chat,
  shout-outs every ~3 h, and joint staged boss runs (`GuildBossRuns.start` takes the allies' members). An alliance is
  betrayed 10% a day: a betrayal megaphone and war.
